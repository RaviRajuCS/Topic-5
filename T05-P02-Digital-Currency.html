Part 2 · Teacher session

1. What is digital currency?

A digital currency is a form of currency that exists only in electronic form. It is represented, stored and transferred using digital systems rather than as physical notes or coins.

Physical cash

Banknotes and coins that can be physically held and exchanged.

Digital currency

Electronic value that is accessed and transferred using digital devices and systems.

How are digital currencies used?

  • Making electronic payments for goods and services.
  • Sending value to another person or organisation electronically.
  • Paying online or using a digital payment application.
  • Holding and managing an electronic balance through a digital service.

Examples to discuss: an electronic balance in a bank account, a digital payment balance, and a cryptocurrency such as Bitcoin. These examples are not all identical: a bank balance is recorded by a financial institution, while cryptocurrencies use cryptographic systems and may use a blockchain.

Important distinction: Not every digital payment or electronic bank balance uses blockchain. Blockchain is one way of recording and tracking certain digital currency transactions.

2. What is blockchain?

A blockchain is a type of digital ledger. A ledger is a record of transactions. In a blockchain, records are grouped into blocks. Each block is linked to the previous block, forming a chain.

Digital ledger

An electronic record used to keep track of transactions.

Time-stamped record

A record associated with the date and time it was added.

Linked blocks

Each block is connected to the preceding block, helping preserve the sequence of records.

Cannot be altered

In the syllabus description, the blockchain is a series of records that cannot be altered. Changes to an established record are designed to be prevented or made evident.

How a blockchain transaction is recorded (simplified)

1 · InitiateA sender requests a digital currency transfer.
2 · BroadcastThe transaction is shared with the blockchain network.
3 · ValidateNetwork participants check that the transaction follows the rules.
4 · GroupValid transactions are collected into a block.
5 · LinkThe new block is linked to the previous block.
6 · RecordThe ledger is updated so the transaction can be tracked.

This is a simplified teaching model. The exact validation and agreement method depends on the particular blockchain system.

Why is blockchain useful for tracking transactions?

  • Transactions are recorded in an ordered, time-stamped sequence.
  • Blocks are linked, making the history of transactions traceable.
  • The ledger is designed to resist alteration, helping participants check the transaction history.
  • It provides a record that can be consulted to follow the movement of digital currency.
Exam-ready explanation: Blockchain is a digital ledger made up of linked blocks of transaction records. The records are time-stamped and, in its basic form, cannot be altered. This allows digital currency transactions to be tracked in sequence.
